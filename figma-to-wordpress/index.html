From Figma to a working site

Layout, responsive behaviour and editing

Spacing, hierarchy and interactions are part of the design. So are the decisions between the supplied screen sizes. I’ll work through those details with you and flag questions while there’s time to resolve them.

Responsive implementation

Layouts built to handle the space between mockups, longer copy and real images. Missing mobile states become a conversation with your designer.

Reusable WordPress blocks

Content tools shaped around what the client needs to publish. We’ll agree which sections can move, repeat or change before I build them.

Shared design rules

Consistent type, spacing, colours and components in the code, so an update doesn’t require fixing the same thing across several templates.

Practical accessibility

Keyboard use, focus states, headings and form labels considered alongside the visual design. Formal audit or compliance requirements are scoped up front.

Agency collaboration

I can work with your designer, project manager and internal developers. We’ll agree on responsibilities, review points and how feedback gets shared.

A clear handoff

The agreed source files, editing guidance and launch tasks, with hosting, deployment and ongoing support responsibilities established before work begins.

The process

Design review, development and handoff

  1. Review the design together

    I’ll look at the page types, reusable components, responsive layouts, interactions and integrations, and flag gaps that affect scope.

  2. Plan the editing experience

    We’ll decide what the client can change, which content gets reused, and how the WordPress setup fits their day-to-day work.

  3. Build, then review in the browser

    You’ll review the implementation against the agreed design. Feedback can cover the details that only become clear on a working site.

  4. Finish the agreed launch work

    We’ll check the layouts, content tools, forms and other features in scope, then complete the handoff or deployment we’ve agreed on.

Getting an estimate

Request a development estimate

A Figma link is a useful start. These details help me give you an estimate you can plan around:

  • The page list, design status and available mobile layouts
  • What the client needs to edit or publish
  • Forms, integrations, migrations or other functionality
  • Who is supplying copy, images and other assets
  • Your target launch date and any fixed review dates
  • Hosting, deployment and technical requirements

If some of that is still undecided, tell me. I can flag what needs an answer before the scope is ready.

Frequently asked questions

Can you join an existing agency project?

Yes. Send me the design, current stage of the project and the part you need help with. We’ll discuss who owns each part of the work, how I’ll collaborate with your team and whether the timing is a fit.

Does the Figma file need to be finished?

You can involve me before the design is finished to discuss feasibility and editing requirements. An estimate will need a clear view of what’s approved and what’s still changing. We’ll agree how design changes during development affect the work.

Do you use Gutenberg or ACF?

Both. The right approach depends on the content, the existing setup and what the client needs to manage. I’ll recommend an editing system that suits the project and explain the tradeoffs before we settle on it.

How do you price development work?

I scope it from the design and requirements. The number of unique layouts, interactions, editing tools, integrations and content migration all matter. I’ll confirm the work and responsibilities in the estimate rather than treating every page as the same amount of development.
